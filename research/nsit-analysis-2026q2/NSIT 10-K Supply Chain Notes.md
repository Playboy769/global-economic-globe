# NSIT 10-K Supply Chain Notes (FY2025 10-K, filed 2026-02-12, acc 0000932696-26-000007, period ended 2025-12-31)

Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/932696/000093269626000007/nsit-20251231.htm

## Item 1, Business — Partner concentration

- During 2025, Insight **purchased and resold products/software from over 6,000 partners**; the majority of purchase $ volume was direct from manufacturers/publishers, remainder through distributors.
- **Purchases from Microsoft ≈32% and TD Synnex (distributor) ≈12%** of aggregate purchases in 2025. No other partner accounted for >10% of purchases.
- **Top-5 partners as a group (Microsoft, TD Synnex, Google, Cisco Systems, Ingram Micro)** = **≈63%** of total 2025 purchases.
- Except for Microsoft, Insight states it is "not dependent on any single partner for sourcing products" because competitive supply sources exist in substantially all product categories.
- **Sales of Microsoft products ≈17% of consolidated net sales in 2025.** No other manufacturer's/publisher's products represented ≥10% of consolidated net sales.
- **Top-5 manufacturers/publishers as a group (Microsoft, Dell, Cisco Systems, HP Inc., Lenovo) ≈50% of consolidated net sales** in 2025.

This is the single most important concentration risk in the filing: Insight is structurally dependent on Microsoft for both procurement (32% of purchases) and revenue (17% of net sales) — directly relevant to the earnings call's repeated emphasis on Microsoft 365 E7/Copilot/Agent 365 momentum and to any "Google partner program changes" headwind discussion (Google is a top-5 purchase partner but not separately quantified as a % of purchases or sales in the 10-K — only named as part of the top-5 group).

## Distribution partners

Named distributors explicitly cited in the top-5 partner group: **TD Synnex** (≈12% of purchases) and **Ingram Micro** (part of the top-5 group, % not separately broken out). **Arrow Electronics is not named anywhere in the 10-K** as a distribution partner (the task brief's assumption that Arrow is a named partner does not hold for NSIT's FY25 10-K — this appears to be an Arrow-is-a-typical-IT-distributor assumption that doesn't match Insight's actual disclosure).

## Client concentration

**No explicit "largest client %" or "public-sector %" figure is disclosed anywhere in the 10-K's Item 1 Business section** — the only client-concentration statement found (repeated in both the 10-K and Q2 FY26 10-Q) is negative-assurance language: *"None of our clients exceeded ten percent of consolidated net sales for the three or six months ended June 30, 2026 or 2025"* (10-Q) / equivalent annual-period language in the 10-K. Client mix is disclosed only at the **category** level (Large Enterprise/Corporate, Commercial, Public Sector) by segment in Note 20/the segment note — see the 10-Q Key Data file's segment tables for $ breakdowns. Public sector as a % of North America net sales in Q2 FY26: $192,452K / $1,938,663K ≈ **9.9%**; EMEA public sector: $77,474K / $375,194K ≈ **20.6%**; APAC: $27,309K / $85,640K ≈ **31.9%**. (These are Q2 FY26 10-Q figures, computed here, not a 10-K-native disclosure.)

## Gross vs. net revenue presentation (agent vs. principal)

Confirmed explicitly in both the FY25 10-K and the Q2 FY26 10-Q segment note: transactions are recognized **gross, as principal**, or **net, as agent**, with the agent treatment specifically called out for **cloud-delivered/SaaS software** ("cloud-delivered software is presented net in agent services revenue" — verbatim from the Q2 FY26 earnings-call transcript, consistent with 10-Q Note 9 disclosure). Q2 FY26 principal-vs-agent split (consolidated): Gross/Principal $2,157,625K vs Net/Agent $241,872K (≈10.1% of net sales recognized net). This is the accounting mechanic behind the "on-premise software declining as clients shift to cloud" narrative on the call — as more transactions migrate from gross hardware/software resale to net-basis cloud consumption, reported net sales growth is structurally suppressed relative to underlying transaction volume, even as gross profit dollars grow. EMEA is a particular hot-spot for large agent transactions: *"the continued contribution from large agency transactions where we acted as a paid pass-through agent in transactions for certain clients and their vendors in the Middle East"* — cited in the Q2 FY26 10-Q as a driver of EMEA Adjusted EFO margin trends, and consistent with the call's mention of "ongoing transactions in UAE and Saudi Arabia, where we act as the agent" as an EMEA growth driver.

## Item 1A, Risk Factors — key supply chain / partner risks

- **Partner reliance risk** (recurring risk-factor heading, cited verbatim in both 10-K and 10-Q cross-references): *"We rely on our partners for product availability, competitive products to sell and marketing funds and purchasing incentives, which can and do change significantly in the amounts made available and the requirements year over year."*
- **Competitor risk**: *"The IT hardware, software and services industry is intensely competitive, and actions of our competitors, including manufacturers and publishers of products we sell, can negatively affect our business."* Insight notes it sometimes competes *directly* with its own publisher/manufacturer partners — explicitly naming **Microsoft, Cisco Systems, Dell, HP Inc. and Adobe Systems** as partners who also sell direct to large enterprise/corporate customers.
- **Supply chain / memory pricing**: Item 1 "Our Backlog" section states supply chain constraints "largely normalized across all product categories as of December 31, 2025; however, entering 2026 we anticipate that this could change for certain categories. **For example, a global memory (DRAM and NAND) shortage is expected in 2026.**" This directly foreshadows the Q2 FY26 10-Q's live "Supply Chain, Demand and Inflation Update" section, which as of Jun 30, 2026 confirms the memory shortage materialized: *"our supply chain is impacted by the global memory chip shortage, which has resulted in lower overall supply and increased pricing and may result in further constrained overall supply and upward pressure on pricing."* The 10-Q additionally flags *"international conflicts, including the war in Iran, may impact supply chain and increase inflation"* — a live geopolitical risk factor not mentioned on the earnings call transcript provided.

## Competitors named (Item 1, "Our Competition")

- **Systems integrators / digital consultants**: Accenture, Capgemini, Atos, HCL Technologies, Tata Consultancy Services, Infosys.
- **Technology providers / value-added resellers / direct marketers**: **CDW, Presidio, World Wide Technology, SHI, Computacenter.**

## Employees / global delivery centers

- Total headcount as of Dec 31, 2025: **14,505 teammates** (North America 11,017 / EMEA 2,759 / APAC 729).
- Offshore service/back-office delivery locations named: **Armenia, India, Moldova, the Philippines, and Romania** (the 10-Q's MD&A separately describes "global delivery centers... across India, the Philippines, and Eastern Europe" in the context of the One Insight operating-model transformation — Moldova/Romania are the "Eastern Europe" locations, so this is consistent, just phrased differently between the two documents).
- Workforce composition: 10,249 sales/client-facing teammates (of which 6,688 are "sales professionals"), 3,745 management/support/admin, 511 distribution.
- No U.S. labor union representation; some foreign works councils/committees exist (e.g., Germany).

## Sales mix by offering, gross profit contribution (FY25 vs FY24, consolidated)

Product (hardware + software) and Services contribution to **gross profit**: 2025 = 41% product / 59% services; 2024 = 43%/57%; 2023 = 46%/54% — a multi-year secular shift toward services-driven gross profit that is directly consistent with the Q2 FY26 quarter's own margin-mix commentary (services margin expansion driving the 60bps gross-margin gain).

## Partner program changes — verbatim FY25 10-K MD&A language (for cross-quarter comparison)

The FY25 10-K's own MD&A repeatedly cites "partner program changes" as a driver of **decreased** cloud-related fee revenue during FY2025 across all three segments — e.g. EMEA: *"a net decrease in fees from cloud solution offerings primarily as a result of partner program changes"*; APAC: *"a net decrease in certain fees from cloud solution offerings primarily as a result of partner program changes."* This is the headwind that both the FY25 10-K and the Q2 FY26 call describe as now largely anniversarying/annualizing out ("we move past the majority of the partner program changes we have previously discussed" — Q2 FY26 call). **Neither the FY25 10-K nor the Q2 FY26 10-Q names which partner(s) drove these program changes** — the specific attribution to Google is a call/slide-deck-only disclosure (see the 10-Q Key Data file's "Partner-program-change language" section).
