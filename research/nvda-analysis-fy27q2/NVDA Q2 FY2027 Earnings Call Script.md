## Management Discussion Section (Colette Kress opening remarks — key figures)

Total revenue $96 billion, more than doubled YoY, growth accelerated for the fourth consecutive quarter. Expect to grow revenue ~70% in fiscal 2028 (supply-constrained outlook).

Q2 data center revenue +18% QoQ to $89 billion. Hyperscale $49 billion, +13% QoQ. ACIE $40 billion, +25% QoQ, +138% YoY. Cloud industry backlog >$2 trillion; top-five hyperscaler CapEx ~$800B in 2026, $1.3T in 2027.

AWS partnership expansion: additional 2 million GPUs starting this quarter through Q2 FY2029, plus Vera CPUs; AWS to serve NVIDIA Nemotron models on Bedrock/SageMaker and adopt the full physical AI stack (Omniverse, Cosmos, Isaac, Jetson) for warehouse robots.

Neocloud partners expected to exit the year with 8 gigawatts installed capacity, up from ~3GW at end of 2025. Revenue-sharing structure with neoclouds described: NVIDIA provides a take-or-pay minimum-revenue guarantee (not a loan), shares in upside above the floor — "paid twice."

Vera Rubin: production shipments commenced this month (August 2026); 30x higher throughput per megawatt, 35x lower token cost vs. Grace Blackwell Ultra; purchase orders received from every major hyperscaler/AI cloud/system OEM. Networking (Spectrum-X Ethernet) +2.6x YoY. Vera CPU in full production; demand ~$20B in standalone server CPUs; preliminary FY2028 expectation for CPU revenue to more than double. Groq 3 LPX rack-scale system in full production (Nebius first adopter this quarter).

Frontier AI labs: ~$50B invested to date; partnerships with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, KKR to raise >$500B third-party capital. SoftBank Energy/Portsmouth (PORTS) campus: 4.25GW initial deployment for OpenAI, ~$1.5M NVIDIA GPUs per generation, 20-year site life. OpenAI's total commitments ~12GW. Selective credit enhancement for ~2GW of compute for "another frontier AI lab." AI-lab-related demand expected to be ~a quarter of NVIDIA's business next year.

China: <1% of Data Center revenue was Hopper (H200) shipped to China this quarter under USG licenses; no China data center compute revenue in forward outlook.

P&L: GAAP/non-GAAP gross margin both 75% (flat QoQ). OpEx +10%/+11% QoQ (compute infrastructure + comp/benefits). Non-GAAP effective tax rate 16%. Inventory $32B (Vera Rubin prep). DSO increased to 60 days (extended payment terms for large multi-quarter investment-grade customer purchases). Capital return: $26B in Q2 ($20B buybacks + $6B dividend at $0.25/share); 60% of FCF returned YTD vs. 50%+ target.

**Q3 FY2027 guidance:** Total revenue $108B ±2%. Vera Rubin ~20% of data center revenue in Q3. Q3 gross margin 74% ±50bp; margins expected to bottom at 71–72% in Q4 before settling at 72–73% in FY2028 as price increases take effect in Q1 — driven by "extreme pricing conditions in memory," magnitude exceeding prior expectations and still rising into next year. Q3 OpEx ~$9.2B GAAP / ~$9.0B non-GAAP. Full-year OpEx growth guided to low-50s%. FY2027 tax rate 16–18%.

Closing: Jensen keynote fireside chat at Goldman Sachs Communacopia & Technology Conference (San Francisco, Sept 10); GTC Berlin keynote (Oct 21). **Q3 FY2027 earnings call scheduled for November 17, 2026.**

## Q&A Section — full exchanges

### Joe Moore (Morgan Stanley)
**Q:** Color on the 70% guide and the confidence to guide a full year out (unusual for NVIDIA); what's the gap to the ~100% demand growth and can it close over time?
**A (Huang):** AI agents use 15–100x the compute of a single human user; ACIE (sovereign/regional/neocloud/AI-native/enterprise) is ~half of the business and growing ~100%/year — likely to exceed cloud computing in scale over time. Standing up infrastructure now requires securing land/power/shell years in advance — hence NVIDIA's upstream (memory suppliers, power generators) and downstream (land/power/shell companies) involvement. "Even though our demand is much greater than 70%, our supply allows us to confidently deliver 70%." Guided a full year for consistency across customers/shareholders/supply chain.

### CJ Muse (Cantor Fitzgerald)
**Q:** Evolving agentic-AI inference workloads and share evolution; ACIE growth and Groq 3 LPX.
**A (Huang):** AI lifecycle now has four phases (data prep, pretraining, post-training, agentic inference) — NVLink-72 rack-scale architecture (3rd generation) is fungible across all four. Revenue opportunity per gigawatt: Hopper ~$18B → Grace Blackwell ~$25B → Vera Rubin ~$40B (five types of networking + Groq). "Each gigawatt of data center increased from, say, $30 billion about five years ago to now $60 billion today." Groq 3: record token interactivity, low latency; bolt-on for high-ASP/high-interactivity services; vast majority of data centers remain Vera Rubin NVLink-72.

### Stacy Rasgon (Bernstein)
**Q:** ~$200B uptick implied by the 70% FY2028 guide vs. prior $1T/3-year outlook — contributors across Vera CPU/Groq/pricing? Unconstrained number?
**A (Huang):** "The unconstrained would be a lot higher. We grew 100% year-over-year this year." Hyperscale backlog $2T; hyperscaler compute is "very profitable" and directly lifts customer revenue/earnings. Reiterated per-gigawatt economics (Hopper $18B → Blackwell $25B → Rubin $40B), framed as a platform shift affecting every computer company/industry.

### Vivek Arya (BofA)
**Q:** ~$500B sum of ecosystem commitments (per CFO commentary) — is that the full picture or more to come? Cash portion in FY2028? How does NVIDIA balance investing in frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) that are also designing competing custom chips (e.g., OpenAI's "Jalapeño")?
**A (Huang):** NVIDIA is a full AI-factory platform vs. single-cloud/single-service XPUs; expects frontier labs to keep running on NVIDIA globally regardless of custom-chip efforts; called the AI-lab investments "once-in-a-generation," regretting not investing "more and sooner"; two of the invested companies likely to go public soon.
**A (Kress):** Supply commitments are the biggest and most essential part of the ~$500B figure, concentrated in the first three years, underpinning Vera Rubin ramp confidence.

### Timothy Arcuri (UBS)
**Q:** Rise of open-source models (e.g., Nemotron) — good or bad for NVIDIA given frontier labs drive much of end demand?
**A (Huang):** Both open and closed models are "skyrocketing"; nearly all open models run on NVIDIA due to footprint/fungibility (PCs, DGX Spark, robots, workstations, on-prem). Open models are foundational for AI start-ups/enterprises building proprietary/domain-specific AI (e.g., cybersecurity). NVIDIA is "the only platform that runs every frontier model," benefiting from success of either category.

### Ben Reitzes (Melius Research)
**Q:** Demand growing 100% next year — how do recursive self-improvement (RSI) and potential AGI (OpenAI targeting by year-end) act as a further demand catalyst?
**A (Huang):** "It's going to inflect further." Most AI is now agentic (crossed this past month per Huang); expects a 40,000-employee company to eventually field 400,000–4,000,000 agents running continuously (cites DGX Spark/DGX Station edge agents running 24/7). Describes current coarse-grained recursive self-improvement (agents updating their own "skill file"/markdown after each run) — argues AGI milestones are "senseless at this point" since useful/profitable AI work is already happening; more compute → more profitable tokens → more profit for services, "the reason why everybody's leaning in."

### James Schneider (Goldman Sachs)
**Q:** Rank the most acute supply constraints (power/shell, DRAM, wafer/foundry, etc.) behind the 70%-vs-100% supply/demand gap.
**A (Huang):** Declined to single out one supplier ("one of the funnest things to do... their stock price doubles the next day" — so declined). Characterized the entire supply chain as "challenged... everybody is really running flat out," with capacity and yields improving continuously rather than in a step-change; reiterated supply ≈70% of demand and the need for "the help of the entire supply chain."

### Aaron Rakers (Wells Fargo)
**Q:** Path of $/gigawatt beyond Vera Rubin (toward Vera Rubin Ultra) — does $40B scale to $60–80B? Is capacity scaling linear or does something unlock further scale into FY2028?
**A (Huang):** Goal is directionally toward "infinity per gigawatt" — historical progression ~$3–5B (general-purpose) → $18B (Hopper) → $25B (Grace Blackwell) → $40B (Vera Rubin) → higher still, provided productivity/durability/fungibility keep rising so customers keep recouping returns fast ("return on investment capital is now less than a year" on $50B data centers).
