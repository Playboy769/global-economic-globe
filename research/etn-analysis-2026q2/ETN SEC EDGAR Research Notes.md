> "Eaton's major requirements for raw materials include iron, steel, copper, nickel, aluminum, lead,

No individual raw-material supplier is named. NOT FOUND: no per-commodity concentration percentage

naming specific suppliers or components.

### 4b. Single-source supplier / third-party manufacturing risk (Item 1A, Risk Factors)

> "We also maintain single-source supplier relationships because either alternative sources are not
> available, or the relationship is advantageous due to certain considerations, such as performance,
> quality, support, delivery, capacity, or price. Unavailability of, or delivery delays for,
> single-source components or products could adversely affect our ability to manufacture or ship the
> related products in a timely manner... We may rely on third-party suppliers for the components
> used in our products, and we may rely on third-party manufacturers to manufacture certain of our
> assemblies and finished products."

This is boilerplate/general — no specific contract manufacturer or component supplier is named by
Eaton in this filing.

### 4c. Customer concentration (Item 1, Business Segment Information subsections)

| Segment | Concentration disclosure | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Electrical Americas + Electrical Global (combined) | 22% of segment sales to **six** large electrical-products/systems customers, 2025 | Item 1, "Electrical Americas and Electrical Global" |
| Aerospace | 20% of segment sales to **three** large aircraft OEMs, 2025 | Item 1, "Aerospace" |
| Vehicle | 37% of segment sales to **four** large vehicle/component OEMs, 2025 | Item 1, "Vehicle" |

⚑ Reading between the lines: Vehicle segment's 37%-to-4-OEMs concentration is the highest in the
portfolio, and eMobility's 18%-to-1-OEM is a genuine single-customer dependency at the segment level
— worth flagging for the risk-matrix tab of the downstream analysis, especially given the pending
Mobility/Dana separation.

### 4d. Competitive landscape (Item 1, per-segment subsections)

- **Electrical Americas / Electrical Global:** "Principal methods of competition... are performance
  of products and systems, technology, customer service and support, and price. Eaton has a strong
  competitive position in these segments and, with respect to many products, is considered among the
  market leaders."
- **Aerospace:** "Principal methods of competition... are total cost of ownership, product and
  system performance, quality, design engineering capabilities, and timely delivery. Eaton has a
  strong competitive position in this segment and, with respect to many products and platforms, is
  considered among the market leaders."
- **Vehicle:** "Principal methods of competition... are product performance, technology, global
  service, and price. Eaton is considered among the market leaders in this segment."
- **eMobility:** "Principal methods of competition... are product performance, technology, global
  service, and price." (No "market leader" claim made for eMobility specifically — notable
  omission relative to the other three segments.)

### 4e. Segment descriptions (abbreviated; full text in Item 1 and Note 18)

- **Electrical Americas / Electrical Global:** electrical components, industrial components, power
  distribution and assemblies, residential products, power quality/connectivity, wiring devices,
  circuit protection, utility power distribution, power reliability equipment; principal end
  markets: commercial & institutional, data centers/distributed IT, industrial, utilities,
  residential, machinery OEMs.
- **Aerospace:** aerospace fuel, hydraulics, pneumatic systems (commercial + military), filtration
  systems; principal customers: commercial/military aircraft manufacturers and aftermarket.
- **Vehicle:** transmissions/transmission components, clutches, hybrid power systems, superchargers,
  engine valves, differentials, fuel vapor components; principal customers: heavy/medium/light-duty
  truck, SUV/CUV, passenger car, construction & agricultural equipment OEMs and aftermarket.
- **eMobility:** high-voltage inverters/converters, fuses, circuit protection, vehicle controls,
  power distribution, fuel-tank isolation valves, commercial-vehicle hybrid systems; same OEM
  customer base as Vehicle.
