## 3. 10-K FY2025 (accession 0001137789-25-000157, filed 2025-08-01, period 6/27/2025, file stx-20250627.htm)

### Manufacturing / vertical integration
- STX designs & manufactures "primarily" its own read/write heads and recording media in-house — core differentiator vs. less vertically integrated peers. Mix of internally-made vs externally-sourced heads/media varies with product mix, tech, internal capacity.
- Glass substrates 100% purchased from third parties; spindle motors purchased from outside vendors (occasional co-design participation).
- PCBAs (ASICs + control chips): mix of in-house design + outsourced contract manufacturing.
- Disk drive assembly & functional test: primarily China and Thailand. Subassembly/component manufacturing: China, Malaysia, Northern Ireland, Singapore, Thailand, United States.
- "There are a limited number of independent suppliers of components, such as recording heads and media" — supplier concentration risk explicitly flagged; certain raw materials/components/equipment are single-sourced (direct and indirect vendors).
- Acquired Intevac Inc. (thin-film processing systems supplier) 3/31/2025 for $119M — aligns with strategy to integrate more of its own manufacturing process equipment (relevant to HAMR media/laser production).
- FY2024 onward: began requiring longer-term demand forecasts/commitments with cancellation charges from key global OEM customers (previously customers could defer/cancel orders largely penalty-free) — tightened supply/demand alignment ahead of the current up-cycle.

### Legal / regulatory (10-K risk factors + FY2026 press release cautionary language cross-check)
- Press release's forward-looking-statement boilerplate references "our ability to make timely quarterly payments under the settlement agreement with the U.S. Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security" — this is NOT discussed anywhere in the prepared remarks or Q&A of the earnings call itself.
- Per BIS public enforcement records (web search, not an SEC filing): BIS imposed a $300,000,000 civil penalty on Seagate Technology LLC (April 2023) for 429 unlicensed exports/re-exports of ~7.4M HDDs (value >$1B) to Huawei in violation of the Foreign Direct Product Rule — the largest administrative penalty in BIS history at the time. Settlement terms: $15,000,000/quarter installments over 5 years (i.e., roughly 2023–2028), plus a multi-year audit requirement and a 5-year suspended Denial Order. This obligation is therefore still running through the period covered by this report and likely into FY2028.
