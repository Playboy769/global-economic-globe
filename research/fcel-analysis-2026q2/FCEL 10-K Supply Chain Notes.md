## (a) Supply Chain / Manufacturing / Key Suppliers

Source: Item 1, "Business" section of the 10-K.

- **Raw materials:** "We use various commercially available raw materials and components to construct a fuel cell module, including nickel and stainless steel, which are key inputs in our manufacturing process."
- **Supplier structure:** "While we manufacture fuel cells in our Torrington facility, the electrical and mechanical BOP [balance-of-plant] components are assembled by and procured from several suppliers."
- **Supplier diversification strategy:** "We continually evaluate and qualify new suppliers as we diversify our supplier base in our pursuit of lower costs, security of supply, and consistent quality."
- No specific single-source/sole-source supplier was named in the extracted text (the risk factor discussion below references dependency on third-party suppliers generally, but the detailed risk-factor paragraph naming specific single-source dependencies was not fully retrievable — see gap note under Risk Factors below).

**Manufacturing facility (Torrington, CT):**
- "We operate a 167,000 square-foot manufacturing facility in Torrington, Connecticut where we produce the individual cell packages and assemble fuel cell modules for our carbonate fuel cell products."
- "As of October 31, 2025, the Torrington facility was operating at a 41 MW per year annualized production rate on a single production shift. Maximum annualized capacity (module manufacturing, final assembly, testing and conditioning) is 100 MW per year under the Torrington facility's current configuration."
- ⚑ Cross-reference: the Q2 FY2026 earnings call (see transcript file) discusses expanding this same Torrington facility from a previously-announced 350 MW/year plan to 500 MW/year, at a cost of $200-275M — i.e., roughly a 5-12x expansion from the 41 MW actual / 100 MW nameplate figures disclosed as of the FY2025 10-K date (Oct 31, 2025). This is a substantial planned step-change versus the as-filed 10-K baseline.

### Commitments and Contingencies (Note 20 in the 10-K; filing exhibit R27.htm)

- **Service Agreements:** Company must maintain fuel cell plants at minimum operating levels for customers; failure to meet contractual minimums may trigger "performance penalties and/or may be required to repair or replace" fuel cell modules.
- **Power Purchase Agreements:** Customers purchase power at negotiated rates; company bears operating and fuel procurement costs; performance-penalty provisions apply if the company underperforms.
- **Project Fuel Exposure:** Fluctuating fuel price risk across several projects. Mitigation: cost-pass-through mechanism at the Bridgeport, CT project; fixed-price contracts at Tulare, LIPA Yaphank, Derby, and Toyota projects; potential financial hedges. Inability to secure favorable fuel terms "may result in impairment charges" to the Derby, Yaphank, and Toyota assets.
- **Derivatives (natural gas):** Gains/(losses) of $4.7M gain (FY2025), $6.9M loss (FY2024), $4.1M gain (FY2023). Derivative assets: $2.0M (FY2025) / $1.2M (FY2024). Derivative liabilities: $0.1M (FY2025) / $4.0M (FY2024).
- **Unconditional purchase commitments:** "$59.8 million" as of October 31, 2025 (materials and services). **No maturity-year breakdown was found in the extracted footnote text.** (Note: this differs from the $77.0 million figure disclosed six months later, as of April 30, 2026, in the Q2 FY2026 10-Q — consistent with new purchase commitments being added as the company ramps manufacturing capacity plans.)
- **Legal Proceedings:** Management believes pending legal matters will not materially harm the consolidated financial statements; no material accruals recorded.
