# JNJ FY2025 10-K — Supply Chain / Manufacturing Notes (filed 2026-02-11, acc 0000200406-26-000016)

Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/200406/000020040626000016/jnj-20251228.htm (Item 1 Business, Item 2 Properties, Note 19 Legal proceedings)

## Confidence note

JNJ's 10-K discloses supply chain structure at a **much lower granularity** than a semiconductor/hardware filer — no named suppliers, no supplier concentration percentages, no named 10%+ customers. This itself is informative: JNJ treats raw-material and component sourcing as diversified/non-material enough not to warrant specific disclosure, **except** for the one area it is forced to disclose in detail — talc, because of litigation, not procurement risk. The "供應鏈" tab below is built from what is disclosed (manufacturing footprint, IP/royalty dependency, third-party manufacturing risk language, talc supply litigation) plus explicit confidence flags where the filing is silent.

## Manufacturing footprint (Item 2, Properties)

- **63 manufacturing facilities**, ~10.4M sq ft total.
  - By segment: Innovative Medicine 5,245K sq ft / MedTech 5,118K sq ft (near 50/50 split).
  - By geography: United States 22 facilities (3,037K sq ft), Europe 22 facilities (5,035K sq ft), Western Hemisphere ex-US 7 facilities (1,054K sq ft), Africa/Asia/Pacific 12 facilities (1,237K sq ft).
  - Within the U.S.: 3 facilities used by Innovative Medicine, 19 by MedTech. Outside the U.S.: 16 Innovative Medicine, 25 MedTech — i.e. MedTech's manufacturing footprint is far more U.S.-weighted in facility count than Innovative Medicine's, which is overwhelmingly offshore (16 of 19 non-U.S.).
- JNJ "generally seeks to own, rather than lease" manufacturing facilities; office/warehouse facilities are often leased. The Company "also engages contract manufacturers" (no names, no % of COGS disclosed).

## Raw materials (Item 1) — verbatim, deliberately generic

> "Raw materials essential to the Company's business are generally readily available from multiple sources. Where there are exceptions, the temporary unavailability of those raw materials would not likely have a material adverse effect on the financial results of the Company."

No component- or material-level detail follows. Cross-reference: Item 1A risk factors carry a generic "Risks related to supply chain and operations" bullet list (manufacturing disruption, IT/vendor breach, reliance on global supply chains) but again with no named dependency.

## Third-party manufacturing risk language (Item 1A)

> "The Company relies on third parties to manufacture and supply certain of our products... we depend on these third-party manufacturers to allocate to us a portion of their manufacturing capacity sufficient to meet our needs... we cannot guarantee that these third-party manufacturers will be able to meet our near-term or long-term manufacturing requirements, which could result in lost sales."

Additional named risks of third-party reliance: loss of regulatory-compliance/quality-assurance control, IP misappropriation, limited inventory management, breach or non-renewal of manufacturing agreements "at a time that is costly or inconvenient."

## IP / royalty dependency (Item 1, "Patents")

- DARZALEX + DARZALEX FASPRO = **~15.0% of FY2025 total company revenue** — JNJ's single largest revenue-concentration line.
- Underlying patent families are owned by **Genmab A/S**; Janssen Biotech holds an exclusive license. This is a upstream IP-supply dependency structurally similar to a component-supply dependency: if the Genmab license terms or patent estate were disrupted, ~15% of company revenue is exposed. Not raised at all on the Q2 call.

## Talc — the one place JNJ discloses a named supplier in detail, because of litigation, not procurement

- **Imerys Talc America, Inc.** (and affiliates Imerys Talc Vermont, Imerys Talc Canada) was JNJ's talc supplier; filed Chapter 11 in Feb 2019. **Cyprus Mines Corporation** (sold talc mines/assets to Imerys) filed Chapter 11 in Feb 2021.
- July 2024: JNJ, Imerys, Cyprus and the tort claimants'/future claimants' committees entered a global settlement (the "Imerys Settlement Agreement") resolving disputes over (i) JNJ's alleged indemnification obligations to Imerys/Cyprus for talc personal-injury claims and (ii) entitlement to proceeds of certain JNJ insurance policies. Approved by the Delaware Bankruptcy Court Oct 2024; insurer appeals of that approval are still pending before the Third Circuit as of this filing.
- This is the supply chain's most material disclosed dependency-turned-liability: a former raw-material supplier's bankruptcy is now enmeshed with JNJ's own multi-billion-dollar litigation reserve (see Risk Matrix tab / Legal proceedings note for the $3.7B current reserve and 76,000-plaintiff count).

## Segment structure (Item 1)

Two reportable segments: **Innovative Medicine** (Oncology, Immunology, Neuroscience, Pulmonary Hypertension, Infectious Diseases, Cardiovascular/Metabolism — sold direct to retailers/wholesalers/distributors/hospitals/HCPs) and **MedTech** (Cardiovascular, Surgery, Vision, Orthopaedics). No distributor-concentration disclosure (e.g. no McKesson/Cardinal Health/AmerisourceBergen-style 10% customer language found in the filing) — flagged as a filing silence, not confirmed non-existence; large pharma manufacturers typically do carry wholesaler concentration in reality but JNJ does not break it out in Item 1 or the notes reviewed.
