**Critical one-off:** H1 FY25 "Other (income) expense, net" of −$7,214M is driven almost entirely by a **~$7.0B talc-litigation reserve reversal** booked in Q1 FY25 (Note 20 / Legal proceedings — see below) after JNJ's three Chapter 11 bankruptcy attempts (Red River / Pecos River / earlier LTL Management) were each dismissed. Q1 FY25 alone: Other (income)/expense = **−$7,321M**; Q3 FY25 alone: **−$478M** (another favorable one-time item, unexplained in transcript). This means H1 FY25 diluted EPS ($6.82) and FY25 full-year diluted EPS ($11.03) are **not comparable** to any period without the reversal — the −35.3% YoY "decline" in H1 diluted EPS (H1 26 $4.41 vs H1 25 $6.82) is a one-off comp effect, not a business deterioration. **This was never mentioned once on the Q2 FY26 call.**

Segment income before tax (as filed, GAAP-consistent per-segment measure) is **lower** than the "adjusted income before tax margin" JNJ cites on calls (IM 42.5%, MedTech 22.0%) because the filed segment measure already embeds intangible amortization ($0.8B IM / $0.5B MedTech in Q2), restructuring, and acquisition-integration costs that the non-GAAP "adjusted" figure excludes. Approximate bridge for IM: 38.1% + amortization (0.8/16.384=4.9pp) + restructuring (0.2/16.384=1.2pp) + acquisition-integration (0.1/16.384=0.6pp) ≈ 44.8% — in the right direction of 42.5% but not an exact reconciliation (other unallocated special items not individually broken out in the filing).

**Unallocated corporate items (footnote 4, critical):** "The fiscal second quarter and fiscal six months of 2026 include **charges for talc matters of $0.4 billion and $0.8 billion**, respectively. The fiscal six months of 2025 includes approximately **$7.0 billion related to the talc reserve reversal**." — JNJ is now booking ~$0.4B/quarter in ongoing talc costs sitting entirely below the segment lines (invisible in segment income before tax), and this was not mentioned anywhere on the earnings call.
